# Start Here: Implement One Complete Data Product Chain

Do not begin by converting an entire estate into contracts or by launching every capability at once. Start with one decision that matters and make its evidence path complete enough to review.

## The pilot chain

1. Register one owned business objective with a baseline and target.
2. Describe one use case and its information need.
3. Record one create, reuse or extend product decision.
4. Publish one validated ODPS product contract.
5. Publish one ODPC catalog reference to that contract.
6. Create one ODPG graph linking objective, use case and product.
7. Define one ODPR workflow for a repeatable validation or onboarding step.
8. Collect one quality or SLA observation against a declared commitment.
9. Measure one adoption signal that is meaningful for the use case.
10. Measure one outcome against its baseline.
11. Hold one portfolio review and record the continuation, change or stop decision.

The [Customer 360 example](examples/customer-360/README.md) shows this chain with a realistic extension decision and separate declared and observed state. Use it as a shape, not a template to copy blindly.

## What makes a good pilot

Choose a product with an identifiable owner, one motivated consumer group, an existing information gap, a manageable access path and an outcome that can be measured within a reasonable review period. A small regulated or high-risk product can also work if there is enough control evidence to learn from it.

Keep the pilot narrow. Existing ticketing, catalog, monitoring and approval tools may remain in place as long as their handoffs and retained evidence are clear. The goal is not a platform replacement. It is a reviewable chain that shows what is declared, what happened and who decides what to do next.

## What to review at the end

Ask whether the product was discoverable, whether access was appropriate, whether the declared commitment was observed, whether the consumer used it for the registered use case and whether the outcome justified continuing investment. If a link is missing, record it as the next improvement rather than inventing a new specification field.
