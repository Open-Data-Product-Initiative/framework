# C1. Strategy and Objectives

## Capability summary

| Capability ID | Function | Purpose | Primary owner | Primary standard | Key outputs | Key evidence |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| C1 | DIRECT | Turn organisational intent into measurable data-product direction. | Objective owner | ODPC where an objective is represented | Objective, KPI and guardrails | Approved objective and review decision |

## Purpose

C1 ensures that a data-product investment starts with an owned business, public-value, risk or regulatory objective rather than available data or a technology project. It makes the expected change, baseline, target and decision owner visible before a product is proposed.

## Why it matters

Without an explicit objective, teams can deliver technically useful assets that no decision-maker is prepared to fund, measure or stop. Later claims of value then have no baseline or accountable sponsor.

## When it applies

Use C1 when a strategic priority, policy obligation, service problem, material risk or value target could create or change demand for data products.

## Inputs

Strategic direction, mandates, current performance, a named owner, beneficiaries, constraints and a proposed baseline and target are needed.

## Practices

Record the objective in outcome language, distinguish it from a delivery activity, name its accountable owner and agree how progress will be measured. Review it when a material assumption changes and retain the reason for changing a target or metric.

## Roles and accountability

The objective owner is accountable for the outcome. Portfolio decision-makers use it to prioritise investment; product owners and analysts contribute evidence; governance participants test applicable constraints.

## Outputs

An approved objective, KPI baseline and target, time horizon, decision guardrails and links to candidate use cases are produced.

## ODPS family mapping

ODPC can represent a BusinessObjective and KPI. ODPG can connect it to use cases and products. ODPS is not the place to invent portfolio-level objective fields.

## Evidence

Evidence includes an approved objective record, owner acceptance, baseline source, review minutes and traceable links to funded demand.

## Measures

Measure the share of funded products traceable to an active objective, objectives with a baseline, and objectives reviewed on schedule.

## Example

The customer-retention director owns the objective to reduce annual churn from 18% to 14% by the end of 2027. The baseline comes from the finance-approved churn report, not from a later model output.

## Questions to ask

- What decision will this objective change?
- Who owns the target and can accept trade-offs?
- What is the baseline, source and measurement period?
- Which use cases could contribute to it?
- What would show that the objective is no longer a sound basis for investment?

## Common failure modes

Teams use a slogan as an objective, measure an activity instead of an outcome, or allow a product team to claim strategic authority it does not hold.

## Minimum implementation

Register one owned objective with a baseline, target, due date and a link to one use case.

## Advanced implementation

Maintain objective-to-portfolio traceability and use outcome evidence, costs and assumptions in regular investment reviews.

## AI-Agent-First extension

Agents may read approved objective context for prioritisation, but cannot reinterpret a target, accept risk or approve investment. See the [AI-Agent-First Profile](../../profiles/ai-agent-first-data-product-profile.md).

## Assessment levels 1-5

Level 1 has isolated statements of intent. Level 2 has named owners and repeatable objective records. Level 3 links active demand to approved objectives. Level 4 measures progress and exceptions. Level 5 uses outcome evidence to adapt portfolio choices without delegating accountable decisions to automation.
