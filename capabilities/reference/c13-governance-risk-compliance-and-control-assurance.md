# C13. Governance, Risk, Compliance and Control Assurance

## Capability summary

| Capability ID | Function | Purpose | Primary owner | Primary standard | Key outputs | Key evidence |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| C13 | ASSURE | Demonstrate that applicable controls operate. | Control owner | Framework evidence; ODPG context | Control applicability and findings | Execution, exception and remediation evidence |

## Purpose

C13 connects a product to applicable policies, risks, controls and approvals, then proves that the controls operated during the relevant period.

## Why it matters

A policy link or a configured control is not evidence of execution. Overstating compliance can hide unreviewed risk and creates a false assurance claim.

## When it applies

Apply it when a product is subject to legal, regulatory, contractual, security, privacy, ethical, financial or internal control obligations.

## Inputs

Product scope, policies, risk assessment, applicable obligations, control owner, evidence requirements, exception authority and remediation timelines are inputs.

## Practices

Determine applicability, map controls proportionately, assign owners, perform and record control activity, assess findings, approve time-bound exceptions and verify remediation.

## Roles and accountability

The control owner is accountable for operation. Product owners provide context; risk and compliance roles interpret applicability; internal assurance tests evidence; designated authorities accept residual risk.

## Outputs

Outputs are an applicability decision, control mapping, executed-control evidence, finding, exception and remediation plan.

## ODPS family mapping

ODPG can connect products to policy and control objects. ODPS may carry applicable product conditions but does not replace a control-management system. Evidence and findings are framework-level operating records.

## Evidence

Keep applicability rationale, control execution, reviewer and period, exception approval, finding severity and remediation verification.

## Measures

Measure controls with current evidence, overdue exceptions, failed controls, remediation age and products with unresolved high-risk findings.

## Example

For Customer 360, approved-consumer access and production-release approval are applicable controls. The observed unresolved entitlement and missing release approval create two findings; an exception exists only if a designated authority recorded one.

## Questions to ask

- Which policy or obligation applies and why?
- What control proves the requirement operated?
- Who owns the control and exception decision?
- Which evidence applies to which product version and period?
- Has remediation been verified rather than merely planned?

## Common failure modes

Teams copy an enterprise control list into every product, equate a policy with compliance, or retain screenshots without owner, date or test method.

## Minimum implementation

Map one applicable control, name an owner and retain one execution record and exception path.

## Advanced implementation

Maintain product-control coverage, test evidence quality and use findings and exceptions in portfolio risk decisions.

## AI-Agent-First extension

Agent actions must be attributable, tool-bounded and reviewable; required human approval remains a hard control rather than a model instruction.

## Assessment levels 1-5

Level 1 relies on informal compliance claims. Level 2 documents applicability and ownership. Level 3 retains operating evidence. Level 4 measures exceptions and remediation. Level 5 uses evidence-backed controls in governed automation and risk decisions.
