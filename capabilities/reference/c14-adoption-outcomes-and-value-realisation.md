# C14. Adoption, Outcomes and Value Realisation

## Capability summary

| Capability ID | Function | Purpose | Primary owner | Primary standard | Key outputs | Key evidence |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| C14 | ASSURE | Judge whether products create justified value. | Product owner and objective owner | ODPC/ODPG context; framework evidence | Value review and portfolio recommendation | Adoption, outcome, cost and attribution record |

## Purpose

C14 evaluates whether consumers adopted the product, whether a supported use case improved and whether the organisation should continue its investment. It separates activity from outcome and outcome from claimed value.

## Why it matters

Usage charts can make an unused or ineffective product appear successful. Without a baseline and attribution limits, value claims become untestable.

## When it applies

Apply it after initial adoption, at agreed value-review intervals, when adoption declines, after material change and before continuation or retirement decisions.

## Inputs

Objective baseline and target, use-case success measure, consumption evidence, product and operating costs, confounding factors, attribution method and review cadence are inputs.

## Practices

Measure discovery, access and meaningful use; compare outcome to baseline; document contribution and uncertainty; include cost; review the evidence with product and objective owners; record the portfolio decision.

## Roles and accountability

The product owner is accountable for product adoption evidence. The objective owner judges outcome relevance. Finance and portfolio governance assess cost and investment; analysts document method and limitations.

## Outputs

Outputs are an adoption measure, outcome comparison, value or contribution assessment, cost view, decision recommendation and review record.

## ODPS family mapping

ODPC represents objectives and use cases; ODPG connects them to products. Runtime systems supply consumption observations. Value and attribution evidence belong in the framework operating record, not invented ODPS fields.

## Evidence

Keep product/version consumption evidence, baseline source, outcome method, cost source, confounders, reviewer decision and known limitations.

## Measures

Measure active use cases, meaningful consumption, outcome movement, value confidence, unit operating cost and products that would not be funded again today.

## Example

At the Customer 360 six-month review, retention analysts used the extension in the churn workflow, but annual churn moved only from 18% to 17.2%. The review records other retention interventions and decides whether further investment is justified.

## Questions to ask

- Who uses this product and for which use case?
- Is use growing or declining?
- What outcome was expected and what was the baseline?
- What changed and what else may have caused it?
- What does the product cost and would we fund it again today?

## Common failure modes

Common failures are counting access requests as adoption, claiming causation from correlation or reviewing value without including operating cost.

## Minimum implementation

Choose one adoption measure, one use-case outcome measure, one baseline and a scheduled portfolio review.

## Advanced implementation

Maintain outcome and cost evidence across the portfolio, state attribution confidence and use findings to create, extend, merge or retire products.

## AI-Agent-First extension

Agent consumption must be attributable to an approved workflow or purpose. High activity does not prove value, and agents cannot self-certify the outcome.

## Assessment levels 1-5

Level 1 counts anecdotal use. Level 2 defines adoption and outcome measures. Level 3 records regular evidence. Level 4 measures cost, outcomes and uncertainty. Level 5 systematically adapts portfolio investment from evidence.
